# P6.5 implementation review and remediation handoff

## Status and authority

This document records the completed static review of the P6.5 implementation
commits after `codex/main` on 2026-08-16. The inclusive review range is
`5783291` through `c9cfe61`: six linear commits matching the six work units
recorded in the implementation roadmap. P6.4 is not part of this range; it is
an intentionally unimplemented conditional unit, as confirmed for this review.

This is review evidence and a remediation handoff, not a second capability
ledger. The [implementation roadmap](../../implementation-roadmap.md) remains
the sole owner of current feature status and acceptance state. The
[Chapter 6 numerical-methods guide](../../../guides/chapter-6-numerical-methods.md)
and [interface specification](../../../design/interface-specification.md)
remain authoritative for complementarity representation, active-set
subproblems, PDAS convergence, and compiler registration.

The history establishes much of the selected algorithmic slice: typed box
bounds and multiplier conversion, disjoint active-set classification,
restriction and prolongation, active equality rows, a PDAS iteration with
separate diagnostics, and a serial cellwise deal.II realization backed by a
positive diagonal mass metric. P6.5 is not acceptance-complete from the
reviewed state. Five P1 subproblem, algebra, compilation, and ownership
defects and two P2 validation or verification gaps remain.

The review preserves historical causality: every diff and its resulting state
were inspected in commit order before evaluating the final state at
`c9cfe61`. Static verification also included a clean `git diff --check` over
the complete P6.5 range. No historical build or test command was run. The
committed test scenarios and roadmap pass counts are therefore evidence of
intended coverage, not an independent rerun of those profiles.

The following behavior is consistent with the selected P6.5 contracts and
should be retained:

- complementarity is an explicit contract and is not hidden inside metric
  projection;
- multiplier classification first converts a covector to its primal
  representative rather than comparing raw dual coefficients;
- finite ordered box bounds, positive finite classification parameters, and
  one-block selected controls are checked;
- active, inactive, lower, and upper selections are represented explicitly,
  with matching restriction and prolongation operations;
- active selections retain their lower or upper bound values, while an empty
  active set reuses the base P6.3 product;
- the base KKT multiplier conversion is preserved and the box multiplier is
  recovered separately from control stationarity, with zero inactive entries;
- each PDAS iteration records the KKT solve, active-set changes, primal and
  dual feasibility, complementarity, stationarity, and equality residuals;
- convergence requires a stable active set, a converged inner KKT solve, and
  every full residual criterion; KKT failure and the iteration limit remain
  separate stopping reasons;
- the serial wrapper owns its mass metric and requires the selected positive
  diagonal coefficientwise realization; and
- continuous, facewise, quadrature-point, mixed-constraint, and state-
  constraint targets remain excluded.

## P6.5-R1 – active coordinates are constrained instead of eliminated from a restricted KKT product

**Severity:** P1 – the implemented active subproblem is a different KKT
product from the restricted free-coordinate product frozen by the interface
specification.

### Evidence

The typed selection exposes `restrict_free()` and `prolong()`, but the active
subproblem uses neither operation. Its primal layout remains the complete base
layout, its quadratic action calls the complete base $Q$ action, and its
equality action calls the complete base $D$ action before appending an active
selection row. It fixes active coordinates by adding equality equations and a
new multiplier block.

`to_base_point()` does not reconstruct a full primal point from free and
active coordinates; the primal was never restricted. It only discards the
appended multiplier block. The resulting algorithm therefore introduces an
extra saddle-point multiplier for the active box row and then independently
recovers the box multiplier from base control stationarity.

An augmented-equality formulation can be mathematically equivalent under
additional assumptions, but it is not the declared P6.5 product. The
authoritative contract requires composition of restricted quadratic and
equality actions, solution in the free primal coordinates, and reconstruction
of the full primal point before reclassification. The two formulations have
different layouts, conditioning, rank assumptions, multiplier meaning, and
solver evidence. The roadmap closure silently changes that frozen boundary by
describing “active equality KKT composition.”

### Required outcome and tests

Build a typed free-primal layout and explicit restriction/prolongation for the
whole KKT primal. Compose the free action and affine terms after substituting
the selected lower or upper values, solve the P6.3 product without adding a
box multiplier to its framework equality-multiplier layout, and reconstruct
the full point before reclassification.

Tests must assert the reduced dimensions and typed block maps, the affine
right-hand-side shifts caused by nonzero active values, exact reconstructed
active and free coordinates, unchanged base multiplier conversion, and
agreement with an independent constrained reference. Retain the empty-set
identity path. If augmented equality rows are desired as a future alternative,
they require a separately declared formulation policy and acceptance evidence.

## P6.5-R2 – the active multiplier contribution overwrites the base transpose action

**Severity:** P1 – the active KKT product does not implement the composed
$D^{\mathsf T}\lambda$ action it advertises, and its forward and transpose
actions disagree.

### Evidence

`ActiveSetKKTSubproblemT::make_active_product()` first evaluates the base
`apply_d_transpose()` action. For each active coordinate, it then assigns the
appended active multiplier directly into the resulting control vector. The
required augmented action is the sum

$$
D_{\mathrm{base}}^{\mathsf T}\lambda_{\mathrm{base}}
+ E_{\mathcal A}^{\mathsf T}\eta,
$$

but the implementation replaces the selected entry of the first term with
the second. A nonzero base multiplier contribution at an active coordinate is
therefore lost.

The separately implemented full transpose action does add the active equality
seed to the existing base control value. Consequently the active product's
`apply_d_transpose()`/`apply_kkt()` and `apply_kkt_transpose()` are mutually
inconsistent even though the product inherits the base symmetry declaration.
That invalidates the algebra expected by the P6.3 solver, especially its
MINRES path.

The neutral active-row test sets only the appended active multiplier; all base
multiplier blocks are zero. It proves that the active row is present but cannot
detect replacement of a simultaneous base contribution. The dense solve is
assembled from the same defective forward action and is therefore not an
independent oracle.

### Required outcome and tests

Add the active contribution to the existing base control value. Then test a
multiplier with both base and active entries nonzero at the same selected
coordinate and assert their exact sum. Add a bilinear action/transpose identity
for the complete augmented product and run it with mixed seeds. The native
active case must exercise the corrected product through the intended symmetric
solver and independently verify stationarity and the recovered box multiplier.

## P6.5-R3 – the selected PDAS target is not a compiler product

**Severity:** P1 – the roadmap closes a registered formulation slice that the
semantic compiler cannot request, validate, manifest, or return.

### Evidence

No P6.5 commit changes the semantic graph, compiler, compiled-product types,
resolved request, or compilation manifest. At `c9cfe61`, those boundaries have
no complementarity, box-bound, active-set, or PDAS concept. The serial tests
construct `ScalarDiffusionReactionModel`, `ScalarDiffusionReactionKKT`,
`SerialCellwiseBoxComplementarity`, and `PDASSolverT` directly. They do not
compile a semantic formulation.

Consequently the compiler cannot record or validate:

- the bound source and selected control block;
- the dual-to-primal multiplier representation and positive diagonal L2
  witness;
- the classification parameter and residual tolerances;
- active-row rank and kernel-positivity assumptions;
- the inner KKT solver and pairing requirements; or
- the explicit exclusion of unsupported control and constraint layouts.

Invalid direct constructions throw generic contract exceptions. They are not
the stable formulation diagnostics required at the compiler boundary. The
missing route also means there is no compiled owner for the P6.3 KKT service,
complementarity service, and PDAS solver as one executable product. This
finding depends on the open P6.3 compiler and typed-pairing remediation; P6.5
cannot be accepted on top of an unregistered or incorrectly paired base KKT
product.

### Required outcome and tests

Add a distinct compiled box-constrained quadratic/PDAS product, or an equally
explicit formulation product, for the one selected scalar target. The resolved
request and structured manifest must contain the decisions above, with owned
executable services and stable negative diagnostics. Compile the canonical DTO
and supported supplied-OTD route through that boundary, and prove that
continuous controls, non-cellwise bounds, unsupported constraint families,
missing metric conversion, and invalid active-set assumptions are rejected
before construction. Do not infer PDAS availability merely because a direct
KKT product and a box object can be instantiated.

## P6.5-R4 – the generic multiplier representation neither owns nor validates its metric

**Severity:** P1 – a public representation can retain dangling conversion
callbacks, while its recorded realization witness is not tied to the callbacks
that classification executes.

### Evidence

`make_metric_multiplier_representation(const MetricT<Backend> &)` returns two
`std::function` objects that capture `metric` by reference. The returned value
does not own that metric and accepts a temporary through its `const` reference
parameter. A representation or `BoxComplementarityT` that outlives the metric
will dereference a destroyed object on its next conversion or classification.

`BoxMultiplierRepresentationT` also stores a `MetricRealisationWitness`, but
`BoxComplementarityT` never inspects it and does not establish that the
recorded witness describes the conversion callbacks. An arbitrary caller can
pair a witness with unrelated actions. The generic contract therefore does
not prove the selected positive diagonal L2 representation it claims to
record.

`SerialCellwiseBoxComplementarity` avoids the immediate lifetime defect by
owning a `shared_ptr<const MassMetric>` before constructing the callback-
bearing complementarity, and it explicitly checks coefficientwise box support.
That useful target-specific protection does not make the public generic
factory safe. Neutral tests also keep their metric alive by local declaration
order and never detach the representation.

### Required outcome and tests

Make the representation retain an owner-bearing metric handle or capture a
self-contained conversion state. Tie the realization witness to that same
owned object and validate the selected positive diagonal L2 capability at the
appropriate construction/compiler boundary. Prevent construction from a
temporary borrowed metric.

Under sanitizers, create the representation and complementarity, destroy all
external metric handles, and then execute both conversions and classification.
Also reject a witness/action mismatch and a metric that lacks the selected
coefficientwise positive-diagonal realization.

## P6.5-R5 – active products and solvers expose borrowed-lifetime traps

**Severity:** P1 – copyable public executable objects can outlive the objects
captured by their callbacks or references.

### Evidence

`ActiveSetKKTSubproblemT` stores its base product by reference. Every callback
in an active product captures `this`, and `product()` exposes the resulting
copyable `Product` by reference. Copying that product and then destroying the
subproblem leaves all of its actions pointing to the destroyed subproblem.
The constructor can likewise bind its base reference to a temporary product.

`PDASSolverT` separately stores the base product and complementarity by
reference. Its constructor accepts ordinary `const` references, including
temporaries, and there is no shared owner or lifetime token in the solver.
The in-function subproblem lifetime used by the current PDAS loop is safe only
because the solve callback is invoked synchronously before the local
subproblem is destroyed. The public types do not enforce that narrower usage.

The committed tests retain every source object in one lexical scope. None
copies an active product out of its builder or executes a solver after its
construction inputs have been destroyed.

### Required outcome and tests

Put the base product, selection, and callback state in shared value-semantic
storage captured by the returned product, so that an active product is
self-contained. Give the compiled PDAS product an explicit owner, or require
owner-bearing shared handles and reject rvalue borrowing at the public API.
Do not rely on caller declaration order.

Add sanitizer regressions that detach and execute an active product after
destroying its builder, and that retain a PDAS solver after releasing the
source handles. Exercise quadratic, equality, multiplier, full KKT, transpose,
residual, conversion, and solve actions after detachment.

## P6.5-R6 – non-finite iterates can pass feasibility and classification

**Severity:** P2 – malformed numerical input is not rejected at the promised
contract boundary and may be reported as an inactive or feasible coordinate.

### Evidence

Box construction rejects non-finite bounds, but `BoxBoundsT::is_feasible()`
does not check the primal value itself. Both comparisons with a NaN are false,
so a NaN coordinate is accepted as feasible. Classification likewise performs
two comparisons on expressions containing primal and represented-multiplier
values; a NaN makes both false and is classified as inactive.

The conversion methods validate only layouts, not finite output. PDAS can
therefore enter residual and maximum calculations with non-finite values.
Those calculations were designed for finite numbers and do not provide a
stable diagnostic for this case. Existing invalid-input tests cover finite
infeasible controls and policy fields, but not non-finite primal, multiplier,
conversion, or solver output.

### Required outcome and tests

Reject non-finite primal controls and multiplier representatives before
feasibility or classification, and reject non-finite converted and solved
values before reporting convergence. Add NaN and both-infinity cases for the
initial control, initial multiplier, conversion callbacks, active KKT result,
and each reported residual family. These failures should become stable typed
diagnostics at the compiled formulation boundary described by `R3`.

## P6.5-R7 – serial acceptance evidence does not prove all closure claims

**Severity:** P2 – the selected native path is exercised, but the assertions
do not independently establish the agreement and diagnostic behavior claimed
by the roadmap closure.

### Evidence

The inactive serial test checks multiplier round-trip, one-iteration PDAS
convergence, an empty stable set, and the aggregate full-residual flag. It does
not solve the same product directly with the P6.3 KKT solver and compare the
primal and equality multiplier, so it does not independently prove the stated
“inactive-box agreement with the unconstrained KKT solve.”

The active serial test first uses a broad-box PDAS solve as its reference,
then verifies that the constrained run stabilizes with one active coordinate
at the chosen bound. It does not assert the final box multiplier sign or
value, zero inactive multipliers, the individual primal/dual/complementarity/
stationarity/equality residual values, or action/transpose consistency. Thus
the algebraic defect in `R2` can remain hidden while both native scenarios
pass their current assertions.

### Required outcome and tests

Compare the inactive PDAS result with an independent direct KKT solve of the
same compiled product, including both primal and equality multiplier blocks.
For lower- and upper-active native cases, independently check box multiplier
sign and stationarity recovery, zero inactive entries, all five reported
residual values and flags, active-set changes, inner-solve status, and the
augmented action/transpose identity. Run those scenarios through the compiled
route required by `R3` rather than through direct model construction.

## Remediation and acceptance order

P6.5 should remain unaccepted until the following sequence is complete:

1. close the prerequisite P6.3 compiler, typed-pairing, and ownership findings;
2. implement the declared restricted free-coordinate KKT composition and
   full-primal reconstruction (`R1`);
3. correct or remove the defective augmented multiplier action and add
   mixed-seed transpose tests (`R2`);
4. add the selected compiled PDAS product, structured manifest, and negative
   formulation diagnostics (`R3`);
5. make metric representation, active-product, and solver ownership explicit
   and verify detached execution under sanitizers (`R4`, `R5`);
6. reject non-finite numerical input and output with stable diagnostics (`R6`);
7. strengthen neutral and native acceptance evidence (`R7`); and
8. rerun the required sanitizer and Debug deal.II profiles, recording exact
   scenario counts and results in the roadmap.

After those outcomes pass, update the implementation roadmap's P6.5 status in
one place. Do not broaden the remediation into P6.4 preconditioners or the
excluded continuous, facewise, quadrature-point, mixed-constraint, or state-
constraint families.
